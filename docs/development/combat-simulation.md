# Combat simulation

`/simulator` is a public, account-free matchup calculator. Selecting two units automatically calculates shooting and melee as separate attacks against the selected defending models. Each side reuses the catalogue picker, roster pricing, selected datasheet query, and loadout editor. It does not save a roster or change a battle. Weapon alternatives, roster-style weapon profiles, summary probabilities, conditions, and both units’ relevant combat rule controls stay visible. Distribution charts appear on hover, keyboard focus, or tap of the corresponding wounds-lost or models-lost number. Full rule descriptions appear in Markdown tooltips on the rule names, independently of their switches or selectors. Range, source, and condition labels remain visible. Rules and buffs sit above the manual conditions and rules controls. No phase selection or run button is required.

## Shared interface and context

`CombatMatchup` is the compact, container-responsive interface for the standalone page, roster editor, and battle dialogs. The standalone page supplies catalogue controls; the roster editor adds a roster-unit selector. Other callers can supply already evaluated `CombatantSnapshot` values and their own unit controls. Each snapshot holds a selected datasheet, model count, and weapon carriers from the same combatant query. The component does not fetch or mutate roster or battle state. A snapshot can also carry active source-derived combat effects, and battle callers supply current surviving models and existing damage.

Faction choices include their catalogue icons. Points sit beside the combatant heading; Loadout sits on the left and the roster editor's model stepper sits on the right, following the unit's permitted sizes. Each combatant displays T, Sv, W, InSv, and FNP together in the shared profile grid; the defender's FNP reflects the configured calculation value. The swap button floats over the divider between units. Swapping attacker and defender preserves both units' loadouts and model counts, resets situational adjustments and attack preferences, and recalculates both phases. Swapping is unavailable while either unit is missing, updating, or failed.

The simulator’s `combatantDatasheetQuery` accepts detachment IDs, the full roster, and the selected pick index. Its server projection evaluates contextual characteristics and granted weapon abilities, including supported attached-unit effects. The simulator consumes those final values and displays their modifier sources. Roster and battle callers must preserve that complete context rather than rebuilding an isolated catalogue pick. `CombatContext` accepts separately resolved shooting and melee conditions, Feel No Pain, and source labels; explicit user adjustments override those defaults, and Reset adjustments restores them. Key the matchup by combatant identity when changing units to reset local adjustments.

The roster editor offers **Simulate combat** in the selected unit’s pane, including the owner’s View mode. Opening it copies the current draft picks and roster settings into an isolated simulator session, including model counts, loadouts, enhancements, detachment IDs, and attachments. The roster side can switch among those picks; the opponent uses catalogue selection. Both sides share the standalone controls, loadout editor, swapping, and results. Simulator edits do not autosave or alter the roster; closing discards them and reopening starts from the current roster. Frozen battle rosters do not offer this action.

Same-unit query placeholders preserve controls and the open loadout editor during edits. A pending edit cancels computation and leaves previous estimates visible and muted until current inputs are ready; recalculation and trial counts have no status labels. An error is shown with a retry action. A different unit resets the matchup. Browser tests pause requests to observe these intermediate states, not just their final results.

## Battle integration

Army controls and each surviving unit card offer **Simulate** to players and spectators. The dialog starts with units from both opposing armies; team battles can select either ally’s army. It reads only the battle view’s frozen picks, never the mutable saved list. Living units on the battlefield are selectable. The full roster remains present for army-wide context; destroyed, embarked, and reserve units cannot supply selectable support effects, and their attachments are removed from the simulation context. A change to live casualties refreshes the open matchup. Simulator choices do not send battle commands or alter CP, loadouts, wounds, or losses.

The model counter starts at the surviving count and can move below roster-building minimums. The wounds counter carries damage on the currently wounded model. Both phases start from that same current health. Uniform loadouts reduce their weapon counts automatically. When casualties leave weapon ownership ambiguous, **Survivors** asks the player which model groups and weapons remain before calculating attacks. The surviving weapon totals are projected into the datasheet and the attack plan together. A bearer-only defence is not assumed to belong to an unidentified last survivor.

Attached leaders and bodyguards remain separate selections, with their inherited effects; combined allocations are not inferred. Stratagem usage records do not identify every target or duration, so source conditions, range, and targeted effects remain explicit switches or choices. The simulator does not assume a recorded stratagem is active on the selected unit. Legacy armies without frozen picks cannot open a matchup.

## Roster buffs

The open rules-and-buffs section filters enhancements, upgrades, datasheet and army rules, stratagems, and attached or nearby support abilities by the selected attacking or defending role. It does not append an unfiltered ability list. The full loadout editor retains all enhancement choices. One POST read expands the roster once and returns the selected datasheet, equipped weapon carriers, and combat rules together. Other units use an abilities-only projection. This request starts alongside pricing; alternative loadouts are fetched only when the loadout editor opens. No battle fold is cached. Explicit stratagem and support-recipient keyword requirements and exclusions filter the candidates, including uppercase source keywords without bold markup. Supported target-keyword restrictions also hide buffs when the opposing unit is ineligible. A stratagem that grants effects to other friendly models can appear for its recipient when an active roster unit satisfies the stratagem’s source requirements. Other restrictions, including range, selected targets, usage limits, and doctrines, remain in the displayed source text for the player to confirm. The simulator does not spend CP or mark an ability used.

Simple supported effects use one switch; conditional variants use a selector. Enhancements use the existing catalogue choices and re-evaluation. Evaluated profile changes are not applied again; their source labels remain on the stats. An already projected defensive characteristic does not suppress other effects in the same rule. Supported passive damage reduction, intrinsic re-rolls, self-affecting auras without unresolved conditions, and attached-leader effects start active. Other situational buffs start off. Each side retains its selections when swapped; only effects relevant to its attacking or defending role are applied. The choices are local to the simulator session.

`combatRuleCompiler.ts` composes complete source clauses into typed effects, without matching card names. Supported families include multiple weapon ability grants, hit and wound modifiers and re-rolls, successful-only and unconditional critical thresholds, conditional AP, weapon characteristics, saves, Toughness, Feel No Pain, damage reduction and halving, and ignoring detrimental hit, wound, and skill modifiers. Effects retain their phase, recipient keywords, target keywords, and named weapon. Named weapon references tolerate an omitted leading “the”. Fixed weapon characteristics replace the starting value before additive modifiers. An upgrade for a weapon that already has an ability checks its original profile and other active sources, excluding the same rule’s own grant. Range conditions stay attached to both the grant and its upgrade. Strength-versus-Toughness conditions and critical-wound AP are resolved for each attack. Army menus expose their combat-relevant named choices without assuming a selection. Enemy status menus preserve their common Toughness penalty and selected plague, applying enemy armour penalties when attacking and enemy hit penalties or cover when defending. Independent board conditions become explicit choices; printed alternatives and named stances stay mutually exclusive. Conditions above bullet lists apply to every enclosed effect, and trailing conditions stay attached to their own bonuses. Passive unconditional effects start active, while targeted, range-dependent, and once-per-battle effects require selection.

Selected support can pair a friendly recipient with a marked enemy. Recipient keywords and exclusions are checked before offering the buff, including exclusions written after the selection range. Movement-phase activation does not restrict an effect lasting until the next Movement phase to one attack phase. These buffs start off and label the range at selection and the selected enemy explicitly; the player confirms activation and source visibility from the rule tooltip.

Attack-stat and ability bullet lists retain their shared recipient, weapon, phase, and conditions. Cumulative bullets combine; alternatives stay exclusive, including named-weapon menus. Modifier-ignore lists retain every supported modifier target. Phase-long incoming damage replacements affect opposing attacks, while one-attack cancellations remain unsupported. Resource-funded upgrades preserve their passive effects and expose the optional upgrade without spending resources. Selected enemy penalties belong to the defending role; friendly attacks against a marked enemy belong to the attacking role. Reinforcement and empowerment buffs require explicit activation.

Recognized non-damage clauses, including Leadership changes, Command-phase healing, usage-token reminders, and CP awards after destroying an enemy, do not suppress a calculated combat effect. Unknown additional effects still prevent the whole rule from calculating. Sentence boundaries prevent later activation or recipient wording from consuming an earlier clause.

`combatRules.ts` applies those effects to profiles and attack plans, and handles selected-target and triggered-support forms. Opposing profile modifiers combine before characteristic limits apply, including opposing auras with the same name. AP improvements that explicitly cannot stack use the strongest applicable improvement. Repeated numeric weapon-ability grants use a dominating value when every possible roll is at least as high. Incomparable instances, such as Sustained Hits D3 and Sustained Hits 2, offer an explicit per-weapon choice instead of stacking. Already projected stats do not erase unprojected effects from the same rule. Conditional Feel No Pain against Psychic attacks or mortal wounds applies only to the matching damage. Both automatic and successful-only critical thresholds can coexist, and automatic hits or wounds cannot become critical.

A rule containing an unknown additional effect is not partially enabled. Only rules with supported effects for the selected role or already applied stats appear as buffs. Unsupported, movement-only, and attack-eligibility rules stay hidden; their source descriptions remain in the development audit. Range and selected targets remain explicit beside their controls. Defensive armour penetration changes the opposing weapon profiles. Damage is halved before flat reduction, rounded up, and then passed through Feel No Pain, with a minimum of one for positive damage. Modifiers are situational extras that never override the datasheet or its rules: modifiers add, the better re-roll, Feel No Pain, invulnerable save, critical threshold and Sustained Hits apply, and a rule-granted situation stays on. `combatAdjustments.ts` applies them to the calculated weapons and target; characteristics clamp to what a roll can use, and a changed flat Attacks or Damage value stays at least 1. Save re-rolls are decided while a pool's saves are rolled, against the group current then, because they happen before any save is allocated. Self-sacrificing abilities, unsupported mortal-wound triggers, per-attack damage replacement, and attack-targeting changes remain outside the calculation.

## Calculation

`src/core/combat.ts` resolves attacks without IO. A browser worker calculates both phases with up to 20,000 seeded Monte Carlo trials each, reducing trials for larger workloads. Identical inputs reproduce identical results. Runtime validation bounds profiles, model counts, dice expressions, and worst-case work per trial. The worker has a 15-second timeout; changing inputs or leaving the view terminates it. Failure in one phase does not suppress a valid result in the other.

`combatLoadout.ts` preserves model ownership before the datasheet combines identical weapon profiles. Named weapon modes use the same normalized wargear key as their carriers, summing weapons across model groups, including shared leader and trooper equipment. `combatPlan` defaults to ordinary ranged weapons, or Close-Quarters weapons when those are all the carrier has. The source rules define Pistol as identical to Close-Quarters; both spellings use the same parsing and selection rules. Monsters and Vehicles can use both groups. Melee uses one ordinary weapon per model plus Extra Attacks; a partial specialist allocation uses the shared weapon for remaining models. Defaults follow catalogue order and are not an optimization claim. Alternative weapons and profile modes can be selected above the weapon profiles. Unknown ownership, inconsistent counts, and multiple ambiguous partial melee allocations refuse a combined estimate instead of guessing. A profile with an unsupported ability blocks the phase when selected.

Supported separate mortal-wound abilities include charge impacts, selected-target shooting abilities, and arcing effects tied to an equipped weapon. They retain their printed D6 outcome ranges, target keywords, model counts, and timing before or after attacks. These wounds spill across defending models, bypass saves and weapon damage reduction, and use applicable Feel No Pain. Weapon damage rolls of 1 can be re-rolled where the rule grants it.

The output counts wounds actually lost, models destroyed, and complete unit destruction. Each attack resolves damage prevention before discarding damage beyond the current model's remaining wounds. Damage does not spill between models, including Devastating Wounds. Targets start at full wounds unless the battle supplies damage on the first model in the allocation order. Models with different Toughness, Save, Invulnerable Save, or Wounds form allocation groups counted from the defender's carriers (05.03). Each weapon resolves as one attack pool: wound rolls use the highest Toughness among surviving models (05.02.01), and save rolls resolve from lowest to highest against the current group (05.04). Groups follow datasheet order until the player reorders them. The source data does not mark which models in a unit are CHARACTERS, so the order is not restricted. Carriers that match no profile or do not account for every model refuse the estimate.

The calculation follows the fetched 11th-edition core rules, particularly sections 05 (attack sequence), 13.08 (cover), and 24 (abilities). Cover worsens Ballistic Skill, capped at 6+, rather than modifying the armour save. Hit and wound roll modifiers are summed with each weapon’s Heavy or Lance bonus before the final ±1 cap. Distinct damage-reduction rules add together; repeated instances of the same source do not. Psychic attacks ignore detrimental Ballistic Skill and hit roll modifiers while preserving separate beneficial modifiers. Automatic hits and wounds are not critical results. With homogeneous target defences and one Feel No Pain value, resolving Devastating Wounds alongside normal attacks from the same weapon has the same outcome distribution as resolving them after its normal damage: successful damage packets use the same damage expression and prevention roll.

Supported weapon abilities are Torrent, Lethal Hits, Sustained Hits, Devastating Wounds, Anti, Twin-linked, Ignores Cover, Psychic, Indirect Fire, Blast, Cleave, Rapid Fire, Melta, Heavy, and Lance. Sustained Hits, Rapid Fire, and Melta accept bounded integers or dice expressions, retaining their per-critical-hit, per-weapon, and per-damage timing. One Shot weapons expose an availability choice. Target-keyword restrictions support alternatives and exclusions. The shared keyword parser handles source spelling variants and is also used by granted abilities and the audit. Duplicate active instances offer a mutually exclusive choice; unknown abilities still disable the affected profile. Characteristics accept bounded integers, D3, D6, and sums such as 2D6+3; an unrecognised expression is refused.

## Boundaries

The simulator reads unconditional, numbered Feel No Pain core abilities and uses the best printed roll. Granted Feel No Pain abilities with a source apply automatically only to units that started with one model, since that projection does not preserve bearer ownership within a squad. An explicit context value or manual adjustment overrides the printed default, including None; Reset adjustments restores it. Conditional rolls and arbitrary army, detachment, datasheet, or defensive prose outside the supported buff templates are not interpreted. Evaluated profile changes are included; further ability effects require supported explicit conditions or remain excluded. Conditions & rules exposes both units' ability text and independent hit and wound modifiers and re-rolls for each phase, cover, and Feel No Pain. Lethal Hits can be declined to attempt a critical wound. Re-rolls only re-roll ones or failures, as selected.

The player decides range, visibility, target eligibility, and whether Heavy or Lance conditions apply. Choices for an already extended aura assume its activation cost has been resolved; the simulator does not deduct CP, damage or heal the attacker, or remove sacrificed models. Abilities that sacrifice an attacking model remain unsupported because weapon allocation would also change. Hazardous damage to the attacker and Precision allocation are excluded. Indirect Fire weapons offer direct shooting, indirect shooting while unobserved or moving, and indirect shooting while stationary with a target visible to a friendly unit. The indirect modes grant cover and prohibit hit re-rolls for those weapons; unmodified rolls below 6 or 4 respectively fail before critical-hit rules or modifiers can make them succeed. Other weapons remain direct. Direct shooting is the default; the player confirms the printed eligibility conditions. The standalone picker has no detachment or attached-leader controls; the roster editor carries its current roster context, and battles carry both frozen rosters plus live casualties.

Unit tests cover scripted roll sequences, independent probability checks, model ownership, automatic weapon selection, and evaluated profile effects. Browser coverage exercises anonymous selection, faction icons, automatic shooting and melee, delayed model/loadout edits, swapping distinct and identical units, defensive values and automatic Feel No Pain, responsive layouts, and JavaScript-disabled first frames. Roster journeys also verify inherited enhancements and attached-unit effects, nested loadout dialogs, roster-unit switching, isolation of simulator edits through close, reopen, and reload, and conditional offensive and defensive buffs.

Probability charts and rule tooltips are checked with mouse, keyboard, and touch, including dismissal inside simulator dialogs. Necron journeys verify charge and arcing mortal wounds, vehicle-only target filtering, and support stratagems requiring another eligible roster unit.

Battle browser journeys cover casualty counts below legal roster sizes, wounded-model allocation, explicit surviving special weapons, both armies' stratagems, swapping, responsive dialogs, and unchanged battle state after simulation.

## Coverage audit

`pnpm catalogue:combat` inventories unique source descriptions across datasheets, enhancements, detachment rules, and stratagems. Use `--faction Necrons` to narrow the inventory or `--json` for descriptions, sources, role classification, recognized wording, and separate no-damage-effect and unsupported statuses. Reports read the fetched snapshot and stay outside Git. Recognized wording measures the rule compiler only: it does not include already evaluated profile changes, intrinsic weapon abilities, or prove that every rule is eligible in a particular matchup.

The inventory also reports every raw weapon keyword variant from shared profiles and unselected equipment, with its parser support and source weapons. This coverage is independent of Jev and does not require inference calls.

The inventory also includes unselected and hidden catalogue upgrades, shared ability groups, core rules, and army rules that no default datasheet prints. These are discovery candidates, not assertions that a current roster can select them. Identical names and descriptions are combined, retaining their sources, scopes, and separately calculated attacking and defending roles.

### Jev shortlist

The combat-rule audit runs on pushes to `main` and after the catalogue update workflow publishes a verified snapshot. Both triggers use the same workflow and S3 cache; audits run one at a time so simultaneous triggers reuse completed judgments. Manual runs are also available through Actions. TypeSafe Jev evaluates every inventory description, including entries the current compiler classifies as non-combat. Two independent Noul questions return the probability that a rule can matter to an attacker or defender. Rules can apply to both roles. Questions describe the receiving unit, so a support aura can benefit an attacker and a penalty to enemy attacks can benefit a defender. They require an explicit effect on damage or kill probabilities after a legal attacker, target, and weapons have been selected. Conditional choices and auras qualify when they contain a damage effect. Pure eligibility, range, visibility, targeting protection, Precision allocation, movement, scoring, resources, healing outside the attack sequence, and fluff do not qualify. Each question fixes the friendly recipient in its attacking or defending role to avoid attributing a penalty to the enemy as a benefit of the wrong role.

The job uploads `combat-rule-shortlist` with the source inventory, a complete `report.json`, a readable `shortlist.md`; a bounded summary appears in the Actions run. A role with probability at least 0.8 and no matching compiler support enters the shortlist. Probabilities strictly between 0.2 and 0.8, and disagreements with recognized compiler roles, enter a separate review queue. Both answers can be no: descriptions with both probabilities at most 0.2 are marked **neither**, including non-combat rules and fluff, and do not enter the shortlist. Each classified row records attacker, defender, both, neither, or uncertain relevance. These provisional thresholds organize development work; they do not establish measured classification accuracy. Source text, probabilities, and model revision remain available for checking every judgment. Model judgments never enable a calculation.

The shared simulator filters rule rows and enhancement choices by actual calculation support, independently of model judgments or cache availability. This applies to standalone, roster, and battle views; ordinary roster loadout choices stay available. Unclassified and changed descriptions stay hidden until their effects are supported or their stats have actually been applied. The Jev shortlist and cached judgments organize development work without adding a runtime dependency.

The audit first runs `pnpm catalogue:combat:eval`, a bounded live check of synthetic positive, negative, conditional, and ownership examples. Confidently wrong labels fail the check; uncertain judgments remain visible for review. This is a regression check, not a representative accuracy benchmark. Changed questions invalidate cached judgments through the existing cache key.

Configure the repository's `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` Actions secret; the audit uses the catalogue publisher's existing S3 settings and secrets. The first successful main-branch audit populates S3, so no local S3 credentials or manual seeding are needed. Subsequent runs classify only missing or changed inputs and rebuild compiler coverage from cached judgments. For a local audit, set the TypeSafe key in the environment, `.env`, or `.env.local`, then run:

```sh
mkdir -p combat-reports
pnpm exec tsx scripts/auditCombatRules.ts --json > combat-reports/inventory.json
pnpm catalogue:combat:shortlist --input combat-reports/inventory.json
```

The development-only `@typesafe-ai/sdk` dependency provides typed questions, cancellation, and bounded retries for connection errors, rate limits, and service errors. The model is pinned to `jev-1.13.0`; `--model` accepts another versioned Jev ID. Successful judgments are cached by the exact source state, question definitions, and model. Compiler changes recompute support gaps from those judgments without paying for classification again. The pipeline restores `.combat-rule-cache` from `combat-rule-judgments/v1/` in the existing S3-compatible catalogue bucket and uploads completed JSON judgments even after a partial API failure. A failed S3 restore stops classification rather than treating an inaccessible cache as empty. Entries persist across workflow runs without GitHub cache expiry; uploads never delete previous judgments. Changes to wording, prompts, or model invalidate the affected judgments. Generated reports and caches are gitignored, and the application requires no TypeSafe credentials.

The audit defaults to four concurrent requests and at most 10,000 uncached descriptions per run; the scheduled job uses 32 concurrent requests. `--concurrency` (1–32) and `--max-requests` (0–30,000) bound that work; `--max-requests 0` rebuilds reports from cached judgments alone. Each request has a 15-second attempt timeout, at most two retries, and a 60-second total deadline. The model receives up to eight source labels for context; the report retains every source. Oversized states remain explicitly unclassified for manual review. Service failures stop new requests and preserve successful results; incomplete runs emit a partial report and exit nonzero. Token counts include successful new judgments, while failed attempts and retries can incur additional usage. The discovery job can fail without withholding an already published catalogue snapshot.

Complete rules coverage needs explicit recipients, phase, conditions, effect ordering, and supported attack mechanics. The current compiler handles complete known clauses and exposes situational choices; an unrecognized additional clause prevents calculation of the whole rule. Extend it from the audit’s source descriptions with tests for eligible and excluded recipients, both combat roles, conditional variants, stacking, and the resulting probabilities. Effects such as sacrifices, per-attack damage replacement, and changes to attack targeting require engine support before a wording template can enable them. Source updates are audited again rather than matched by ability name.
