# Catalogue data: Saved lists and interchange

## Saved lists and interchange

Saved rows contain `RosterPick` choices rebuilt against the current catalogue. Writes atomically advance the user-scoped revision; realtime transports carry no private picks. Discovery and profile readers never receive picks, notes, preparation or a variant's base; [Product design](../../product-design.md) owns visibility.

Setup edits use `RosterSetupDialog`'s two-step review; `src/core/rosterSetup.ts` owns the transition. Price the exact candidate through the ordinary construction authority and recheck before confirmation; Cancel and Back save nothing. Account confirmation compares the normalized source inside the durable document transaction so another tab cannot silently rebase the review; saves outside the durable runtime (including the native v3 bridge) use the server expected-version check with one retained operation receipt and need a fresh review after an intervening edit. Guest localStorage is not atomic across tabs. Cover in `e2e/roster-setup-review.spec.ts`.

Unit notes are owner-only: `readerRosterFromRow` and battle/league reads strip them for other readers, rendered notes carry `ph-no-capture`, and no telemetry reads them. `normalisePicks` omits notes so typing never reprices. Legacy `nickname` and `prep.shareNotes` are stripped without changing pending operation inputs or receipts.

`src/core/rosterLock.ts` lets a locked roster accept only visibility changes; `saveRoster` and queued `syncRoster` apply it before the version check, so stale or offline work gets a refusal kept for review. Locking, unlocking and archiving are explicit server operations that need a connection, first sync that roster's queued edits, and advance its version. A lock is not a permission: sharing, export, battles, leagues and deletion are unaffected. Cover in `e2e/roster-lock.spec.ts` and `e2e/roster-archive.spec.ts`.

Variant groups (`baseRosterId`) cannot be changed by saves and outlive their deleted base. A visitor keeps one browser draft, listed on the rosters page; making a second asks them to sign up rather than replacing it. Signing up from that prompt saves the draft to the account under its own ID, so a battle that froze it still links to the saved list; a later-found draft asks before entering an account, and claim retries reuse its ID. Never overwrite an existing visitor draft through an import, a copy or an exchange link.

Imports accept supported text exports (Praetorium, New Recruit, BattleBase, the official app, ListForge's detailed and WTC layouts, Warmind including its ListForge, tournament and official-app texts, and WTC-style text, parsed in `src/core`), not `.ros`/`.rosz`. A faction named by its keyword, such as `Heretic Astartes`, matches through `catalogueFactionName`. App-layout chapter exports name the parent faction before the army immediately above the priced detachments; import the army and read the disposition after those detachments. Verify the New Recruit chapter case against [`rosterText.blackTemplars.fixture.ts`](../../../src/core/rosterText.blackTemplars.fixture.ts), including its allied Assassin and five attachments. Unknown factions refuse; unmatched units or choices are reported for confirmation, never silently dropped or substituted, and every import is reviewed before saving. Show reported points separately from the authoritative price, and report unsupported battle sizes instead of guessing.

Import matching treats typographic hyphens as ordinary hyphens and ignores trailing `(one per army)` and `(Upgrade)` annotations on enhancement labels. Model names accept singular/plural forms and omit a catalogue model's `Squad` or `Sodality` suffix; wargear names accept singular/plural forms with the option's actual weapon count. Within a datasheet, a name matches the catalogue's name that differs from it only by spaces (the app's `Relic Greataxe` is BSData's `Relic Great Axe`), a model named for what it carries (`Wulfen w/Death Totem`) carries it, a model line naming the datasheet plus a title the catalogue lacks (`Thunderwolf Cavalry Pack Leader`) is a model, and a model named by its kind alone becomes the variant its distinctive wargear marks (an `Eradicator` with the multi-melta is the `Eradicator with Multi-melta`). These aliases do not choose a differently named datasheet. Official-app attached groups connect both `Leader` and `Support` entries to their explicit `Bodyguard`; catalogue rules validate the resulting attachment. Verify the Necrons support case against [`rosterText.necrons.fixture.ts`](../../../src/core/rosterText.necrons.fixture.ts), transcribed from the official app’s export preview. The app may list a model's further wargear under one bullet on unbulleted counted lines, and a paste copied from below the title opens with the setup paragraph; verify both against [`rosterText.spaceWolves.fixture.ts`](../../../src/core/rosterText.spaceWolves.fixture.ts). Verify official-app Custodes imports against [`rosterText.custodes.fixture.ts`](../../../src/core/rosterText.custodes.fixture.ts), including the Achillus's paired Adrathic weapons, upgrades, model counts and attachments. The official app translates its labels, so `fromBattleBaseText` reads its layout by structure rather than by English words: points in any locale's grouping, a battle size and units priced in the title's points word, the bodyguard last in its attached group, and the one uncounted, unlabelled bullet as the Warlord. A disposition named in another language takes its matched detachments' disposition, or is reported when they differ. Verify translated text against the German [`rosterText.krieg.fixture.ts`](../../../src/core/rosterText.krieg.fixture.ts).

`src/shared/rosterFiles.ts` exports only readable Praetorium text through `toGwText`. It retains model counts, equipment, enhancements, Warlord, attachments, setup, detachment points, roster entry numbers and rules-version identity. Exports never carry notes, preparation, waived or optional rules. Points alone never choose a battle size they do not equal. Cover exports and their import round trip in [`rosterFiles.test.ts`](../../../src/server/rosterFiles.test.ts) and `e2e/interop.spec.ts`.

Imports without explicit model counts use the models their lines name, else the largest stated wargear count, or the unit's minimum size. ListForge lists each model's wargear under its model line, and the reader keeps that line (`model` on a selection), so a choice on a named model reads only its own lines. A list naming every body in a group by kind fields exactly those models. A piece a counted choice places is not also given to another choice, so a squad's Blast Pistol is not also the leader's.

[`rosterText.fixtures.ts`](../../../src/core/rosterText.fixtures.ts) holds genuine ListForge and Warmind exports, with their provenance; [`rosterTextImports.integration.test.ts`](../../../scripts/rosterTextImports.integration.test.ts) imports them against the snapshot. Replace a fixture only with another genuine export. Previously exported Praetorium compact text remains importable.

`src/core/rosterExchange.ts` owns versioned exchange codes. A roster with a custom budget is written as format 2 so older apps refuse it instead of importing the standard total. Owner, visibility, preparation, reminders and notes are never encoded, and the QR link carries the code in its fragment to keep rosters out of server logs. Run `e2e/roster-exchange.spec.ts` after changing the format or review.

### Saved loadouts

A saved loadout (`unit_loadouts`, [`unitLoadouts.ts`](../../../src/core/unitLoadouts.ts)) is an account's named copy of one configured unit. It differs from a favourite (a datasheet, not a configuration), a variant (a whole roster) and Duplicate unit (within one roster). `loadoutOf` keeps the datasheet, model count and wargear, and drops the note, Warlord, attachment, enhancements and detachment upgrades, because they belong to the army the unit stood in; only a priced unit says which choices are enhancements, so an unpriced one cannot be saved. The picker offers a loadout only to rosters of its army family, in any rules version or detachment. `loadoutPick` moves it into the roster's rules version through `copiedPicks` ([`rosterCopies.ts`](../../../src/core/rosterCopies.ts)), the mapping a conversion uses, and keeps choices the destination lacks so the roster's ordinary pricing reports them for reselection, as it reports unit and enhancement limits. The picker lists each loadout beneath its datasheet ([`pickerRows.ts`](../../../src/client/features/rosters/builder/pickerRows.ts)): searching its name or its datasheet finds it, the filters judge it as its datasheet but with its own price from the roster's pricing, and a loadout whose datasheet the rules version does not offer is listed last with the reason rather than dropped. Its datasheet row states a unit limit before the add, and its menu renames it or deletes it after a confirmation. Renaming changes only the name, under the same validation as saving; names need not be unique. Saving, renaming and deleting need a connection, and saving stops at `UNIT_LOADOUTS_MAX`; adding one is an ordinary roster edit, and deleting one leaves units already added. [Product design](../../product-design.md) owns visibility. Cover in `e2e/unit-loadouts.spec.ts`.
