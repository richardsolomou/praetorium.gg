Roster grades show what a roster can do on the table, one job at a time. Choose **Grade this roster** from the roster's **⋮** menu. Each job gets a grade from A to D, the overview at the top names what the list does best and worst, and **Worth knowing** lists the gaps. The grades are not a chance to win: the player, the mission and the dice decide far more than the list does.

## What each grade measures

- **Board presence** counts your units, each with whatever is attached to it, against the five objectives on the table. Eight or more is an A, six or seven a B, five a C and fewer a D.
- **Even spread** is the share of your points in your two costliest units. Under 40% is an A, under 50% a B, under 60% a C.
- **Reach** is each unit's average chance to charge an enemy at the edge of its deployment zone by battle round two, using its Move, Scouts, Infiltrators, Deep Strike and charge bonuses. The gap is 20″ at King of the Colosseum and 24″ otherwise.
- **Deployment options** is the share of your units that can deep strike, infiltrate or scout.
- **Clear hordes**, **Kill elite infantry**, **Kill vehicles**, **Kill heavy armour** and **Kill monsters** count the units that can, on their own, take half the wounds of 20 Termagants, a Chaos Terminator Squad, a Rhino, a Leman Russ Battle Tank or a Carnifex in one turn of shooting, or shooting then fighting. Heavy armour is left out at King of the Colosseum, which allows nothing above Toughness 9.

Reach, deployment options and the kill rows are graded against the lists that placed at 11th-edition tournaments, by the share of your units that can do each job, so an A means the list is ahead of most of them. Board presence and even spread come from the table itself, so they hold at every battle size. At King of the Colosseum, where each side may field only one Toughness 9 unit, one unit that can kill vehicles or monsters earns a B and two earn an A.

## Gaps

A gap is named when nothing in your list can take half of a reference target on its own, something nearly every tournament list can do. A gap is worth knowing about, not always worth fixing: a list may give up one job for speed, bodies or another strength.

## Where it stops

- Each unit fights at full strength with its saved wargear and its passive rules. Stratagems, conditional rules and abilities you switch on in the simulator stay off.
- A rule the simulator cannot calculate is never guessed. Its row says **Not calculated for** and names the unit instead.
- Reach ignores terrain, transports and the opponent's own moves.
- The card needs a connection, since Praetorium calculates it.
