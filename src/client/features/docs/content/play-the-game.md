The main button ends the current phase (**End the command phase**, **End the movement phase** and so on), then reads **Pass the turn** from the end phase. On a phone, switch between **Your side**, **Battle** and **Opponent**.

Every player has their own colour, shown on their name across Praetorium and in battles, where a side's score blends the colours of everyone on it. Choose yours under **Player colour** in [account settings](/profile), or leave it on **Automatic**. If two players' colours are too alike, such as two blues or a pair a colour-blind player can't tell apart, the player who joined the battle later gets a different one for that game.

## Scoring

In a guided battle, ending a phase where a card scores opens a scoring prompt. Press what the board paid on each card, or **Did not score (or chose not to)**. The next player's turn waits until this is finished. **Nothing scored** answers every card you haven't touched. What you've pressed stays if you leave the battle to check a rule and come back, even without a connection, until the scoring is confirmed or undone. Every phone at the battle shows the same presses, and any of them can carry on. At the end of the fifth round, the final prompt ends with **Finish battle**.

Each payout shows a plain-language summary of what it asks for, beside when it scores and how many VP it pays.

![The end-of-turn scoring prompt listing the primary and two secondary cards, each with its awards and a Did not score row.](screenshot:track-a-battle-scoring "Did not score (or chose not to), under a card's awards")

Tactical cards are drawn when a turn needs them, at random or by selecting exact cards. Discarding tactical cards at the end of your turn gains 1 CP; once your side has gained its extra CP for the round, the turn ends without asking, unless a card's beacon unit was destroyed.

When a card asks you to pick objectives, they're named from the deployment: each player's **home**, the **centre**, an **expansion** nearer one player's zone, and a **flank** as near to both. On six-objective layouts, the centre pair is named by the side each sits on. Left and right are as seen from the zone of the player choosing.

Once a round's last turn is scored, the screen shows each side's victory points with primary, secondary, battle ready and CP, what the round added, and the score of every round so far. Press anywhere to continue. When the battle ends, the same screen shows the result. Each device shows a round once, and anyone watching sees it too.

In a manual battle, each side has **−1**, **+1** and **+5** buttons for primary and secondary, and **−1** and **+1** for CP.

## Command points and stratagems

Guided battles give each side its Command phase CP automatically. **+1 CP** records your one extra command point for the round, then reads **CP gain used**. Discarding a secondary for CP uses the same allowance.

To use a stratagem, press its cost button, for example **2 CP**. If the cost depends on the board, the **⋮** beside it lets you spend a different amount. A greyed-out stratagem shows why on hover: wrong phase, wrong turn, not enough CP, or its use limit is already spent. A stratagem usable in any phase is never refused for its phase, and one whose turn differs by phase, such as your opponent's Shooting phase or either player's Fight phase, is checked against the phase you're in.

On a phone, the CP figure beside the phase button opens the stratagems you can play now, for whichever side's tab is open, with **+1 CP** beside the command points at the top. Use the buttons at its foot to switch sides. Swipe sideways across the battle to move between **Your side**, **Battle** and **Opponent**.

## Casualties

Open **Units** under a player's name to see their units. Remove models and wounds as they go, or mark a unit **Destroyed**. A unit with more than one kind of model, such as a sergeant, a plasma gunner and troopers, lists each kind with its wargear, so you remove the model that actually fell and track its wounds on its own row. A squad whose only difference is one model carrying an upgrade, such as a Chaos icon, lists that bearer on its own row too. A specialist's row is named by what it carries, such as **Plasma gun** beside **Shock Trooper**. Press a row's name to open the unit's datasheet at that model's wargear; the unit's wargear counts only what its models left standing carry, and wargear none of them carries is struck through. Units in reserves or transports show **Arrive** or **Disembark**. A unit's datasheet offers the options it chooses between during the battle, such as an Order or a Doctrina Imperative, to show its profiles as they leave them ([rules you choose during the battle](/docs/set-up-a-unit#rules-you-choose-during-the-battle)). Your opponent doesn't see your picks.

**Roster** opens the list exactly as they fielded it, with its points and wargear, even when they keep the saved roster private. Players and spectators can both open it.

**Simulate** opens the [combat simulator](/simulator) with the units still standing.

## Threats

**Threats** under an opponent's name lists their surviving units, furthest-reaching first. Each row shows the unit's **Move**, its longest ranged weapon's **Range**, and the **Total** of the two. An attached unit counts as one unit, at the Move of its slowest model. The list does not count Advance, charges, terrain, Fly or other abilities, and it shows no rules text. A unit in reserves or a transport shows where it is and has no total, since its reach depends on where it arrives or disembarks. A destroyed unit is left out. A list attached before battles kept loadouts shows its units without numbers.

## Notes, timer and reminders

**Battle note** records a rules call or house rule in the battle log, up to 500 characters. Anyone who can watch the battle can read notes.

**Battle options** holds **Pause timer** (shared by the whole table), **Action reminders** for your mission cards, and the ways the battle ends. The app reminds you of Shooting-phase mission actions as your Shooting phase begins.

## Chess clock

The scoreboard shows how long each side's latest turn took. To give each side a time limit, choose 60 to 120 minutes under **Chess clock** in setup. The scoreboard then also shows the time each side has left, and counts past zero in red when a side runs out. Running out of time does not change the score. When a tournament, hidden-list game or league sets the chess clock for every battle, setup shows its time and you can't change it.

With a limit set, **Hand the clock over for a reaction** under the phase button gives the clock to the other side, such as while they use a stratagem or fight in your turn. **Hand the clock back** returns it. Ending the phase also gives the clock back to the side taking the turn.
