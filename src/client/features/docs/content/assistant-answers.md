All of this works without an account. Ask your AI assistant, such as Claude or ChatGPT, in plain language and it works out what to look up.

## Look things up

Ask about missions, rules, detachments and datasheets by name, rule number, keyword, weapon or ability. The assistant finds the best match and reads the full record. If a name is ambiguous, it can narrow the search to one faction, mission pack or rules document.

- "Using Praetorium, what's the Toughness and Save of a Space Marines Intercessor squad?"
- "Which detachments do Aeldari have, and what does each enhancement cost?"
- "What's the deployment and terrain layout for this mission in the current mission pack?"
- "In Chapter Approved, which mission do I play with Take and Hold against Purge the Foe?"
- "What changed in the last Necrons points update?"

For a mission matchup, the assistant gets both players' primary missions, the deployments and the terrain layouts for that pairing, the same as the matchup page. It can also look up a force disposition and the detachments that offer it, and help you choose fixed or tactical secondary missions against your opponent from the secondary cards' scoring.

Praetorium gives assistants a guide for each kind of task, such as building, importing or grading a list, planning a matchup, choosing secondaries or tracking a battle, and tells them to read it first. Praetorium's data is 11th edition, so assistants are told to take every rule and number from it rather than from what they remember of earlier editions. Some assistants also show these guides as ready-made prompts, such as **Build a roster** and **Plan a matchup**.

## Plan an army

> Using Praetorium, plan a 2,000-point Necrons list around two units of Immortals, and tell me which detachments suit it.

Before it starts, the assistant reads Praetorium's list-building guide: how detachments, detachment points, Force dispositions, enhancements and attachments fit together, and to read the army rule and each detachment's rules before choosing one. It can compare every detachment your faction can take, with its detachment points, Force dispositions, rules and enhancement costs, and see what each one gives a unit you name. It can see each unit's costs, composition, attachments, limits, roles and keywords. Give it a battle size and it uses the limits for that size. Give it a detachment and it also gets that detachment's rules, enhancements, upgrades and stratagems by name, cost and timing. Legends and Crucible datasheets are left out unless you ask for them. It can pick your Warlord, enhancements and wargear, and if you're signed in it can build from the models in your collection.

Paste a list from the official app, New Recruit or another builder, or an exchange code, and the assistant brings it into Praetorium and tells you what didn't match, the same review [Import a list](/docs/import-a-list) shows. Nothing is saved unless you ask. It can also give you a list's text export or exchange code to send to an organiser.

The assistant can check a list with Praetorium before it shows it to you: the same points total and problems the builder shows, without saving anything. It can also grade the list the way the builder does, with a score and grade for board control, mobility, damage and durability, then try swaps for the weakest parts and grade the list again so you can see what changed. Give it your opponent's army and both Force dispositions and it can plan the game: it runs every unit in your list against every unit in theirs, the same as the simulator's [roster comparison](/docs/compare-rosters), then works out which of your units answer their threats and which play the mission. See [Roster grades](/docs/roster-grades) for what the grades measure. To change a list yourself, open it in [Rosters](/rosters). See [Start a list](/docs/start-a-list) and [Import a list](/docs/import-a-list).

## Simulate combat

> Using Praetorium, how many Intercessors do ten Necron Warriors kill in shooting, and what if the Intercessors are in cover?

The assistant can run Praetorium's combat simulator for one attacking unit, with up to two attached leaders or support units, against one defending unit. It reports shooting, melee, and shooting followed by melee, with expected models destroyed, wounds lost and the chance to wipe the unit. Every result links to the same matchup on the [simulator](/simulator) page.

You can ask for the modifiers the simulator page offers, such as cover or having charged, and ask it to switch a rule or weapon profile on or off and run it again. Datasheet rules that always apply are on by default. Stratagems and conditional rules stay off until you ask for them. A modifier doesn't switch on a rule: if a rule needs the unit to have charged or stayed still, ask for that rule too.

It uses each unit's default loadout unless you ask it to use a unit from one of your saved rosters, which brings that unit's wargear. Each unit starts at full strength unless you tell it how many models or wounds a unit has lost. It refuses damage it can't place exactly, such as models lost from a squad with mixed wargear. The simulator link opens every unit at full strength. Where a model chooses between weapons or weapon profiles, it uses the one that does most against the defender unless you ask for another. Name a detachment for either side and it also applies that detachment's rules and offers its stratagems. Otherwise it uses none. With a detachment named, it can also give a character one of its enhancements. It can add units standing nearby, such as a character whose aura reaches the attacker, and switch their auras on. Ask which wargear is best against a target and it searches the unit's legal loadouts the way **Optimize** does on the simulator page, and says when it couldn't check them all. See [Set up a matchup](/docs/set-up-a-matchup).
